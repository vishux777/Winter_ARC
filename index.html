Recovery and progression

You are only 2–3 weeks into this schedule. Build consistency before adding lots of volume.

Current phase: Keep the listed sets for the next 2 weeks. The plan is more challenging through exercise quality, controlled tempo and progression—not by taking every set to failure. Do not add optional sets yet.

Effort, tempo and rest

  • Compound lifts: finish most sets with 2 reps in reserve (RIR); rest 2–3 minutes.
  • Isolation lifts: finish with 1–2 RIR; rest 60–90 seconds.
  • Tempo: lower the weight under control for 2–3 seconds when specified. Do not make reps so slow that technique breaks down.
  • Paused reps: pause briefly at the hardest safe position; no bouncing.
  • Warm up 5 minutes and do 1–3 progressively heavier warm-up sets before the first heavy lift.
  • No forced reps, drop sets or failure on compounds.

Double progression

  • Stay within the listed rep range.
  • When every set reaches the top of the range with clean form and the target RIR for two sessions, increase the load by the smallest available increment.
  • Typical jumps: about 1–2.5 kg upper body and 2.5–5 kg lower body, depending on the exercise and equipment.
  • Record weights and reps. Repeating a weight with better control is progress.

Recovery basics

  • Sleep 7–9 hours.
  • Eat enough and hit protein targets, including Tuesday.
  • Hydrate throughout the day.
  • Tuesday is full rest; easy walking is fine.
Reduce training if: strength falls across several sessions, soreness persists beyond 48–72 hours, sleep or motivation deteriorates, resting heart rate is unusually elevated, or joint/tendon pain develops. Sharp or persistent pain is not normal soreness.
Deload: After roughly 4–6 hard weeks—or sooner if recovery worsens—take one easier week: about half the working sets, 3–4 RIR, and skip optional accessories.
