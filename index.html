Raise protein to 150–170 g

  • Add a second whey scoop or a shake after the evening session (+24 g).
  • Paneer, 100 g in the sabji or as a snack (+18 g).
  • Soya chunks, 30 g dry in the sabji (+15 g).
  • Greek or hung curd, 200 g instead of some lassi (+15–20 g).
  • Roasted chana or sprouts as a snack (+8–10 g per 50 g).
  • Eggs: 4 to 6 a day is fine for most healthy people.
  • Spread protein across 4 meals, about 30–40 g each.

Control oil and portions

  • Measure cooking oil. One tablespoon is about 120 kcal, and it adds up fast in sabji.
  • Use a teaspoon or spray for sabji, around 1–2 tsp per katori.
  • Count rotis the same way every day. Changing from 8 to 6 or 10 is a 150–250 kcal adjustment.
  • Use the same katori size and fill it level.
  • Choose plain curd or lassi without sugar. Salted lassi is fine.
  • Fill half the plate with vegetables and salad to stay full.

Supplements

  • Creatine monohydrate: 3–5 g daily, including rest days. No loading phase needed. Mix into water, milk or lassi, any time of day.
  • Early weight gain is likely water stored inside muscle (about 0.5–2 kg). It is not fat, and it is not a reason to stop.
  • Magnesium: check the label for elemental magnesium, not the compound weight (for example, 500 mg magnesium citrate is only about 80 mg elemental). The general upper limit from supplements and medications is 350 mg elemental magnesium per day unless a doctor advises otherwise. Taking it in the evening is common. Loose stools mean the dose is too high.
  • Whey is a convenient way to hit protein targets, not a requirement. Food comes first.
  • Check with a doctor first if you have kidney problems or take regular medication.

Cardio and steps

Support fat loss without eating into recovery.

Your cardio plan

  • 2–3 indoor sessions per week on a stationary bike or elliptical. They are already placed in your evening sessions: Wednesday (easy), Friday (Zone 2) and Sunday (easy).
  • Start at 8–12 minutes, and add about 2 minutes per week until you reach 20 minutes. Hold there.
  • Daily steps: 8,000–10,000, if recovery allows. Get up for a 3–5 minute walk every hour of your desk work.
  • Running stays paused while outdoor air quality is poor. Check the AQI. Stay indoors for cardio when it is unhealthy.

What is Zone 2?

Zone 2 is an easy, steady effort. You are breathing a bit harder but can still speak in full sentences. A rough guide is 5–6 out of 10 effort, or around 60–70% of max heart rate (about 120–140 bpm at your age). If you cannot talk, slow down. Because it is easy, it burns calories and builds your aerobic base without wrecking your next lifting session.

Recovery and progression

12 sessions a week is a lot. Protect the recovery side.

Early adaptation warning. Two weeks at 12 sessions per week is still an early adaptation period. Soreness, tiredness and joint stiffness are normal at first. Do not add extra sets, finishers or extra days. Do not automatically increase weekly volume. Hold the plan steady and let your body catch up.

Effort and rest

  • Heavy compound lifts (bench, squat, RDL, close-grip bench): stop with 1–2 reps in reserve. Rest 2–3 minutes.
  • Isolation exercises: also 1–2 reps in reserve. On the final safe set only, 0–1 RIR is okay occasionally.
  • Isolation rest: 60–90 seconds.
  • Do not train to failure on every set. Failure builds fatigue far faster than it builds muscle.
  • Warm up 5 minutes, then do 1–2 lighter ramp-up sets before your first heavy lift.
  • Short on time? Pair two isolation exercises back to back.

Progressive overload

  • Pick a weight that lets you hit the lower end of the rep range with 1–2 reps in reserve.
  • Double progression: when you reach the top of the rep range on all sets with good form, add the smallest weight jump next session (about 2.5 kg for upper body, 5 kg for lower body, or one pin).
  • Write down weights. If you cannot beat last time, repeating it is still fine.
  • Progress one or two lifts at a time, not everything at once.

Recovery basics

  • Sleep 7–9 hours. This matters more than any supplement.
  • Eat at least 3 solid meals and hit your protein every day, including Tuesday.
  • Drink water through the day. Aim for pale-yellow urine.
  • Use Tuesday for real rest, with easy walking only.
Warning signs — reduce volume or skip an evening session: strength dropping across several sessions, soreness lasting past 48–72 hours, resting heart rate noticeably higher, poor sleep, low mood or motivation, getting ill often, or sharp or persistent joint or tendon pain. Pain is different from soreness. See a doctor or physio if it does not settle.
Deload recommendation. Take a deload week every 4–6 weeks, or sooner if the warning signs appear. For one week, keep the same exercises but do about half the sets and stay 3–4 reps from failure. You can also drop the evening sessions for that week. Then resume normally.
